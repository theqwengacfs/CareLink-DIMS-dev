# 13 · CareLink DIMS v4.1 — release report

**Version 4.1.0 · schema 5 · 19 October 2026**

This report covers both implementation increments delivered today: the first (sections A to G) and
the second (section H), which answered the additional development instructions.
Built from the v4.0 single-file application, using the v3.3.0 handoff as the architectural reference
and the refinement specification as the requirement set.

---

## A. Changes completed

### A1. The host computer is now the system of record

The v4.0 build stored everything in the browser. That was sufficient for a one-laptop demonstration
and wrong for everything else: a tablet and a laptop could not see the same patient, and the
"backup" was a manual file download.

`server.py` is a new, dependency-free server (Python 3 standard library only) that:

- serves the application and its assets;
- holds one authoritative database in `data/carelink.db` (SQLite, WAL, `synchronous=FULL`);
- gives every accepted change a monotonically increasing revision;
- writes the superseded revision to `data/revisions/` as a gzipped snapshot before overwriting;
- keeps its own append-only, hash-chained audit of commits, conflicts and resolutions;
- refuses a save made against a stale revision;
- de-duplicates retried saves by client operation identifier;
- probes a local model endpoint on behalf of the browser, and refuses any endpoint outside the
  private network.

The client (`src/sync.js`) is a small state machine with an explicit status shown in the header:
`Central database · revision n`, `Saving`, `Needs attention`, `This device only`, `Not saved
centrally`. When two copies exist it never merges: the person chooses, and both copies are preserved.

### A2. Landing page and sign-in are separate screens

- A navy and gold public front page: the Foundation identity, **One Foundation. One Operating
  System.**, the tagline *Serving our Neighbours in Love*, the mandate *Saving Lives, Preventing
  Orphans*, and one primary action: **Proceed to My CareLink Console**.
- It fills the viewport without scrolling, including a tablet in landscape at 700 px height.
- No patient data, no counts, no console fragments ever appear on it.
- Sign-in is a distinct white page with a route back, and signing out returns to the front page.
- The application palette moves to the Foundation navy `#1F3864` and gold `#C9A227`. Departmental
  accents remain, because departmental identity is part of the specification.

### A3. Care plans and the 30-day review cycle

- Enrolment is refused without an initial care plan: identified need, expected duration, the reason
  for that estimate, objective, intervention and responsible officer.
- Twelve review slots fall at 30-day intervals from the programme start.
- A review records both the date it was **due** and the date it was **completed**. A late review
  stays visible as late; editing the patient record cannot reset it.
- A patient beyond the approved programme maximum is flagged **extended** and needs a written
  extension review (20 characters minimum) before discharge. A discharge guard enforces this in the
  workflow, not the interface.
- Reviews due and overdue appear on the caregiver home, the MCM home, the PDCA console and the
  patient record.

### A4. Structured conditions, with real withholding

- Twelve condition definitions with classification and a sensitivity flag.
- Six statuses: suspected, documented, confirmed, historical, resolved, requires follow-up.
- A caregiver may record a suspected condition. Only `condition.confirm` may document or confirm a
  diagnosis, and the confirmation is signed.
- Sensitive conditions (HIV, TB, co-infection, cancer, cryptococcal meningitis) are withheld from any
  role without `condition.sensitive`, including from aggregates.
- Aggregates suppress groups of fewer than five as a band, never a number, and say how many
  categories were suppressed.

### A5. Delegation

- A named colleague may receive a defined set of capabilities for at most 120 days.
- Only capabilities the delegating officer holds may be delegated; the grant is intersected with the
  delegator's live permissions at the moment of use.
- County-scoped officers can only delegate within their county.
- Expiry is automatic; revocation is immediate and audited.
- Actions under delegation are recorded against the delegate's own identity, with the delegating
  officer named. No shared passwords.

### A6. Care centre console

A distinct operational workspace, not a second hospital console:

- bed board and occupancy from 12 beds;
- residents linked to a patient record, never duplicated;
- three-shift daily handover with written entries;
- restricted safeguarding register, readable only with `care.incident`, closable only with a written
  resolution;
- care plan and conditions reachable per resident, subject to clinical capability;
- no theatre lists, no hospital billing, no clinical notes in the operational log.

### A7. Settings workspace

Thirteen labelled sections replacing four dense cards:

1. System information · 2. My preferences · 3. Users, roles and permissions · 4. Organisation ·
5. Patient programme · 6. PDCA and follow-up · 7. Notifications · 8. Reports and indicators ·
9. Local AI assistant · 10. Data protection and security · 11. Backup and integrity ·
12. Network and devices · 13. Appearance and accessibility.

Global configuration is separated from personal preference. A person sees only the sections their
role permits. Global changes record the previous and new value in the audit trail.

### A8. Reporting

- Two new reports: **Donor Programme Summary** (aggregate reach, support delivered, resources, with
  an explicit limitations block and no identifying detail) and **Condition Summary** (with
  suppression).
- A new `charts.js` provides accessible SVG bar charts with a written summary, a key and a full data
  table beneath every chart. No chart relies on colour alone.
- Condition register, donor summary and care centre views added to the navigation, each gated by
  capability and scope.

### A9. Local AI configuration

- Mode, endpoint, model name, timeout and context policy in one section.
- The connectivity test runs **on the host**, so a tablet never needs direct access to the model
  service, and the test cannot be pointed at a public address.
- A test only reports success when the endpoint actually answered, and names the models it found.
- Cloud providers remain locked off, with the reason stated.

### A10. Compliance with the standing rules

- **278 em dashes and en dashes removed** from source, user-facing and otherwise, per the v3.3.0
  ground rule.
- Demonstration names changed from `Demo — Name` to `Demo - Name` for the same reason.
- Every new string is British/Kenyan English.

| # | Defect | Cause | Fix |
|---:|---|---|---|

| 7 | Signing out returned to a sign-in panel rather than the front page | No screen state | `APPX.screen` and an explicit reset on logout |
| 8 | A stale schema on the central database could be silently loaded | No schema comparison | The client refuses a newer central schema and tells the person to update |
| 9 | The lint suite could not see hyphenated view names | Regex `[a-z_]+` | Widened to `[a-z_-]+`; drill-down views declared |
| 10 | Test harnesses loaded a hard-coded module list | Duplication | One shared `test/modules.js` |

## C. Security and data-integrity findings

**Closed**

- Confidentiality of the patient register is unchanged and still enforced in the repository layer.
  The new views (care centre, conditions, donor) all read through `repo` or `RBAC.patientVisible`.
- Sensitive conditions are withheld at row level and at aggregate level. A role without
  `condition.sensitive` sees neither the row nor its contribution to a count.
- The server cannot be walked out of its directory: dotfiles, `data/`, `src/`, `test/`, `tools/`,
  `docs/`, `examples/`, `server.py` and unknown extensions are refused.
- The AI probe refuses any non-private endpoint, so it cannot be used to make the host reach the
  internet.
- Delegated authority cannot exceed the delegator's own authority, at issue or at use.

**Open and documented**

- `server.py` has no authentication of its own. It is designed for a trusted office LAN and for the
  demonstration build. Production must put it behind the application's session layer over TLS, on a
  segmented network, as described in `docs/10_DEPLOYMENT.md`. This is stated in the server's own
  docstring and in the kit README.
- The whole database travels as one JSON document per save. That is simple and auditable, and it is
  the right shape for a caseload of hundreds, not tens of thousands. Above roughly 5,000 patients,
  move to per-record endpoints before scaling further.
- Browser storage remains the working buffer when no server answers. It is not encrypted at rest.
  The honest statement to staff is: use the server, and do not leave a patient record on a shared
  tablet.

## E. Database changes

- `schema` moves from 4 to 5. The migration is additive: six new collections (`patientConditions`,
  `careBeds`, `careResidents`, `careLogs`, `careIncidents`, `delegations`), one new settings block
  (`programme`, `pdca`, `care`, `notifications`, `accessibility`, `sync`), and one new map
  (`personalPrefs`).
- Existing installations keep every record. `DB.load()` tolerates missing collections; every reader
  defaults to an empty array.
- `meta.serverRev` and `meta.serverSaved` are stamped when a device is in step with the host.
- The server database is new in this release; there is no migration from a v4.0 server because none
  existed.

## F. Outstanding work

- **No on-device verification.** Tablet layout, touch targets and the landing page's above-the-fold
  behaviour at a real 800×1280 viewport have not been seen in a browser.
- **`server.py` was tested by HTTP, not by a browser.** The client's state machine is unit-tested;
  the wire protocol is integration-tested; the two have not yet been observed working together in
  Chrome on the office Wi-Fi.
- Native DOCX/XLSX export, in-browser workbook parsing, multi-device offline queues, the restore
  executor, bulk staff import and OCR remain unbuilt and unclaimed.
- The care centre's legal protocols (infection control, medication handling, fire safety) are
  recorded as configurable practice, not as verified Kenyan legal requirements. The policy
  dependency is stated in the console.
- Media library versioning remains flat; the console records versions as metadata, not as retained
  files.
- A dedicated Care Centre Manager role is not invented. See decision B29.

## G. Version and release status

**4.1.0, schema 5.** Ready for a supervised demonstration on one computer, or on a host computer with
one or two tablets over the office Wi-Fi. **Not** ready for real patient data: the server has no
authentication layer of its own, the browser buffer is not encrypted, and no device test has been
performed.

The honest release statement is: *this is the demonstration and acceptance build of one integrated
system, with the central-database model in place and every control tested in code.*

## H. Second increment: personalisation, communication and the public portal

### H1. Ten landing-page themes

- Ten complete front-door designs, not ten recolours: Heritage navy, Dawn, Clinic white, Editorial,
  Canopy, Signal, Sandstone, Violet hour, Paper and Spectrum.
- Each has its own palette, typography and arrangement. Dawn centres the hero and removes the side
  panel; Editorial is a serif single column on paper; Paper is a ruled documentary page; Spectrum
  carries a visible-light ribbon and a gradient headline; Signal is high-contrast black with a
  spectrum rule.
- The System Administrator selects, previews and applies from **Settings, then Landing page and
  public portal**. A preview renders the real page into a frame without saving anything.
- The choice is stored in `settings.landingTheme`, survives restart, and falls back to Heritage navy
  if the stored value is unknown.
- A landing theme cannot change a workspace colour, a status colour, permissions, records or
  workflows. Workspace accent, density and accessibility remain separate settings.

### H2. The sixty-second rule, enforced twice

- The interface counts inactivity from genuine events only: pointer, key, touch, wheel and route
  changes. Background polling does not count.
- A warning appears at 45 seconds with a drain bar and a **Stay signed in** action.
- At 60 seconds the session ends, the interface returns to sign-in, open dialogs close, the
  assistant history is cleared and an audit event `session.expired` is written without any clinical
  detail.
- The host enforces the same rule independently. `server.py` issues an unguessable session token,
  records the last genuine activity, and refuses every protected read and write once the window has
  passed. A heartbeat carrying `active:false` deliberately does not extend the session, so a polling
  tab cannot keep a session alive.
- Tested: expiry on the host, refusal to revive an expired token, extension by real activity,
  immediate refusal after sign-out, and every session being cleared when the server restarts.

### H3. Staff profiles and personal preferences

- A person may maintain a photograph, full name, preferred display name, job title, professional
  designation, work telephone and email.
- Photographs are restricted to PNG or JPEG, limited to 120 KB, checked by content type rather than
  extension, and stored as image data only. A person may remove their photograph.
- Role, department and ministry areas are displayed but not editable from the profile: they are
  organisational records, changed through the registry.
- Preferences already in Settings (density, reduced motion, rows per page, default console) are
  stored against the person and never audited as system changes.

### H4. Presence and internal messaging

- Presence is derived from a heartbeat the signed-in person sends, not from whether a window is
  open. Online, away, offline and busy are expressed with a text label as well as a colour.
- Direct and departmental conversations, unread counts, timestamps, participant-only visibility and
  an audit event on every send.
- A message containing a patient code, a clinic number, a phone number or a patient name is refused,
  with a pointer to the protected workflow. This is a narrow rule: ordinary coordination is never
  blocked.
- A local check flags potentially abusive language for human review. The flag never acts, never
  disciplines and is visible only to the reviewer role. Nothing is sent to any model.
- Messaging works with no server at all. Presence across devices requires the office server.

### H5. Public portal

- A visitor reaches **Visit LOMAMO** from the front page with no account and no password.
- It shows only items that are both `published` and marked public. The filter is applied in
  `publicItems()` for the interface and again in `/api/public/content` on the host, so an unpublished
  item is absent from the data rather than merely hidden.
- Categories, search, a featured item and an archive. No patient record, staff message, financial
  record or internal report is reachable from the portal, and there is no shared staff account.
- The portal is clearly labelled as a demonstration: its seeded items are illustrative and say so.

### H6. Media studio and editorial workflow

- One register with seven states: draft, review, approved, scheduled, published, unpublished,
  archived.
- Approval, publication, unpublishing and withdrawal require the publication authority. A patient
  story must be linked to a patient with recording, unwithdrawn storytelling consent; the rule is
  re-checked at the moment of publication, not only when the item was created.
- Withdrawing consent pulls a published item back to unpublished and records why. Nothing is deleted.

### H7. Calendar, registry and Public Relations

- **Calendar**: visits due, approved hospital appointments, staff leave, work-plan deadlines and
  scheduled publications, drawn from the records rather than a second calendar table. Sensitive
  events show a reference and area, never a clinical detail.
- **Organisational registry**: counties, ministry areas, departments, roles and staff assignments in
  one place, with seven live integrity checks (duplicate names, orphaned assignments, accounts
  without a role, roles nobody holds, duplicate staff) and a recent-change list from the audit trail.
- **Public relations**: stakeholder directory, public enquiries with a reply record and an optional
  information request to Data, Media or Finance, and the communication queue awaiting approval.
  Public Relations holds no patient, ledger or clinical access.

### H9. What is still not done

Stated plainly, because the alternative is a claim that a demonstration does not support:

- **Clinical prescriptions and pharmacy stock** are not built. The clinical module has encounters,
  conditions, the medication reference, interaction checking, calculators and usage reporting; it
  does not yet write prescriptions or hold stock. This is the largest outstanding item.
- **Intelligent scheduling** is not implemented. The calendar shows commitments; it does not propose
  rosters. No AI-generated schedule exists to be mistaken for an approved one.
- **Leave management** remains the existing workflow with the published rules. Balances are not
  computed from service dates, and supporting documents are recorded by reference, not stored.
- **Dashboard widget arrangement** is not customisable. A person can choose density, default console
  and rows per page; they cannot rearrange panels.
- **No device has been used.** The 800 by 1280 tablet layout, the touch targets and the ten themes
  have been rendered in a headless DOM and inspected as markup, not seen on a screen.
- **No browser has run against the server.** The protocol, the session expiry and the decision table
  are each tested; the complete path from a tablet through Chrome to SQLite has not been observed.
- The 60-second rule will be disruptive in real field use. It is implemented as specified. During a
  supervised demonstration the host window can be started with `CARELINK_IDLE_SECONDS`, and any
  change to that value is an exception requiring written approval, as the requirement states.
